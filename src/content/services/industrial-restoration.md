**Industrial water damage, fire damage, or structural saturation in a Little Falls facility?** A burst sprinkler line, a failed roof drain, or a scorched conveyor section doesn't just damage a building, it stops production. The longer equipment sits wet or soot-laden air circulates through ductwork, the more it costs in downtime, corrosion, and ruined inventory. Heritage Restoration LLC scopes industrial losses with an eye toward both the structure and the operation running inside it, so you know what has to stop and what can keep moving.

## What Industrial Restoration actually involves

Industrial restoration covers the same core disciplines as residential work, water extraction, structural drying, soot and odor removal, but at a different scale and under different constraints. A warehouse with a saturated slab under racking systems behaves differently than a house with wet carpet. A manufacturing floor with electrical panels, hydraulic lines, or raw material storage adds exposure categories a typical drying plan doesn't account for. Equipment often needs to be moved, shielded, or shut down before any extraction starts, and large open floor plans mean psychrometric readings have to be taken in multiple zones rather than one room. Depending on the loss, this work pulls from our IICRC WRT, ASD, and FSRT training, and timelines run longer than a residential job simply because of square footage and the number of affected systems.

## Our process

1. **Facility assessment and production impact triage.** We walk the affected area with facility management to identify what's structurally damaged, what equipment is at risk, and which zones can stay operational during the work.
2. **Containment and equipment protection.** Before extraction or soot removal begins, we isolate the work area with poly barriers and negative air where needed, and protect machinery, inventory, and electrical systems from moisture or airborne residue.
3. **Extraction, drying, or soot removal.** Depending on the loss type, this means high-capacity extraction and structural drying monitored with moisture meters, or soot characterization and HEPA cleaning for fire-related losses.
4. **Equipment and materials decontamination.** Machinery, shelving, and stored product are cleaned or assessed separately from the building structure, since corrosion and residue on equipment often outlast visible water or smoke damage.
5. **Documentation for business interruption and insurance review.** We photograph and log moisture readings, affected square footage, and damaged equipment so your claim reflects both property loss and downtime.

## What separates a good industrial restoration response from a bad one

The most common mistake on industrial losses is treating the building like a house, drying the slab and walls while ignoring what's sitting on top of it. Pallet racking, electrical conduit, and HVAC returns can trap moisture or soot long after the floor reads dry. Adjusters reviewing industrial claims look for documented readings across the full affected area, not a single hallway reading applied to an entire warehouse, and they look for evidence that equipment and inventory were inspected separately from the structure. A response that skips equipment-level documentation often results in secondary claims for corrosion or contamination that surface weeks later, which is harder to tie back to the original loss.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Central Minnesota winters bring frozen and burst pipes to unheated or partially heated industrial spaces, often in sections of a facility that aren't checked daily. Spring thaw adds roof and foundation water intrusion risk, especially in older brick and steel-frame buildings common around Little Falls industrial corridors. Humidity swings between seasons also matter for facilities storing paper, textiles, or food product, since those materials pick up moisture faster than concrete or steel and can mold within days if drying is delayed.
